Phase 2 — Denial management
Days 14 through 30
ERA/EOB review — categorize every denial code immediately
CO = contractual obligation, cannot bill patient. PR = patient responsibility, bill the patient. OA = investigate. CO-45 is a normal contractual adjustment — it is not a denial.
Triage — work highest-value claims first, fastest deadlines first
Work claims over $200 immediately. Batch claims under $50 weekly. Maryland Medicaid appeal window: 90 days from denial. Medicare: 120 days. Most commercial: 90 to 180 days from denial date.
Phase 3 — Appeals
Days 17 through 60
First-level appeal — write and submit within deadline
Include patient and claim identifiers, denial code, clinical justification with CPT-ICD10 linkage, relevant LCD/NCD references, and supporting documentation. Set a 30-day follow-up reminder.
Peer-to-peer review — your strongest lever as Medical Director
Request a peer-to-peer call with the payer medical director for CO-50 denials. Present clinical rationale directly. Win rate on P2P reviews in primary care: 60 to 80 percent. Must schedule within 10 business days of denial.
Phase 4 — Patient collections
Days 30 through 90
First statement — mail and portal within 30 days of EOB
Include service date, CPT description, amount billed, insurance paid, patient responsibility, and a clear payment link. Most patients pay on first statement if it is clear and readable.
Phone outreach and payment plan offer — Day 45
Text reminder first, then call within 3 business days. Offer payment plans for balances over $150. Exercise community judgment for Northwest Baltimore patients.
